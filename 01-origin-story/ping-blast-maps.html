Who took pings outside their home base

After 4.2, 86 callouts were taken by someone away from home. Before 4.2, none were. All 86 were in the home areas of three responders who had gone quiet. Line thickness is the number of callouts accepted.

51 of the 86 were taken by the responder pinged first. In 35 cases the first responder declined or missed and the callout passed to someone else. Vesper, Farlight and The Undertow were rarely the one asked, and took 5, 2 and 3 of the callouts in their own areas.

Callouts nobody took

Callouts where no ping was accepted, by area where the callout happened.

Before 4.2, 48 of 879 callouts went untaken. After, 49 of 440. In both periods, most of these (42 of 48, and 40 of 49) had only one ping sent, so the chain stopped after a single responder. That is worth asking Engineering about, since the routing code is meant to keep offering down the list.

Least impacted: Foundry Row. Missed pings rose only from 2% to 10%, the lowest after 4.2, its home responder was still pinged for every callout, and few callouts went untaken. Most impacted: Uptown, Old Town and Harborside (shaded orange), where the home responder was pinged for only 19 to 28% of callouts and outsiders took 86. Riverside, Southport and Kingsbridge (shaded red) now leave 21 to 26% of callouts untaken, and their home responders are the ones covering the three quiet areas. That may be coincidence, with 16 to 57 callouts per area after 4.2.

How this was built

Weekly rates divide the counts by 6.29 weeks (before, 29 Jun to 11 Aug) and 3.71 weeks (after, 12 Aug to 6 Sep). Home area comes from the responders table. Meteor Mite and The Gale share Eastgate. This shows what happened, not why. The cause is unproven: the data has no response times, ranking scores or availability status.
